# BattleScene product specification

BattleScene helps organizers run robot combat meetups across dedicated weight-class arenas. Managers set up the event, run matches, and control venue graphics from a web dashboard. Teams and spectators follow the event on phones and venue screens.

## Roles and meetup lifecycle

| Role | Capabilities |
| --- | --- |
| Super admin | Authorize and revoke meetup managers, manage any meetup, transfer ownership, confirm result rollbacks that affect matches already started, and fill a meetup with demo data. |
| Meetup manager | Create a meetup, manage meetups they own or are assigned to, and add authorized managers to help run them. |
| Team | Register robots for a meetup through its registration link, then edit or cancel them. Sees only its own robots and has no staff access. |
| Spectator | View published event information through a shareable link. |

Staff and teams sign in with Google. A Google account without a staff role can only use team registration pages. The deployment grants super admin access to the Google account specified by `BOOTSTRAP_ADMIN_EMAIL`. Each meetup has its own name, date, timezone, data, and links. A manager can publish a prepared or ongoing meetup, unpublish it, or mark it complete and leave the results online. Marking a meetup complete asks for confirmation and clears its activity log to save space; reopening it does not restore the log. The meetup's owner or a super admin can delete a meetup from **Meetup settings → Delete meetup** after typing its name to confirm. Deleting removes the meetup record and its uploaded images, purges the spectator cache, and records a **Meetup deleted** entry in the staff-access history that super admins see in **General settings**.

The main menu separates running the event from setting it up. **Meetups** comes first. **Run the event** contains **Overview**, **Check-in**, **Arenas**, and **Brackets**. **Set up** contains **Robots & classes**, **Displays**, and **Meetup settings**. **General** contains **General settings**. Every item except **Meetups** and **General settings** needs a meetup to be open. **Check-in** is one desk for every class: staff search by robot or team, filter by class or to robots not yet here, check robots in or out, and withdraw robots once their class has started. **Arenas** runs each class's arena (timer, last call, results, and battle queue). **Brackets** generates and edits each class's bracket, corrects results, and shows standings. **Robots & classes** adds, renames, and removes weight classes and their arenas and holds each class's registration roster (register, paste, rename, remove, seed order, export); it shows arrival status, but check-in happens in **Check-in**. When adding a class, staff can pick a common UK class name (Antweight 150 g, Beetleweight 1.5 kg, Featherweight 13.6 kg, Heavyweight 110 kg); picking one also suggests the next free arena name. **Meetup settings** holds the open meetup's details (name, date, timezone, battle and last-call timings, display language, and **Mark meetup complete**), **Team registration**, its assigned managers, its own activity log (the meetup keeps its latest 500 changes), and **Delete meetup**. **General settings** is always available, even with no meetup open: super admins manage **Global manager access** there, and every staff member sees one activity log across all the meetups they manage, newest first. For super admins the log also records when manager access is granted or revoked. Every staff button shows a tooltip on hover or keyboard focus that says what it will do (or why it is unavailable), and battle status labels such as **Ready** explain what the status means. **Meetups** lists each meetup the organizer can manage with its date and status and provides **New meetup**. Selecting or creating a meetup opens its **Overview**, where the organizer can refresh it and publish or unpublish it. The managed views show the current meetup name and date once. The class selector identifies the active weight class.

## Registration and arenas

The target meetup has 20–30 teams and one to three weight classes. A team can enter several robots, including more than one in the same class. Each class has a dedicated arena and bracket. Managers name the class and its arena and choose an elimination format when adding the class. In **Arenas** and **Brackets**, the class selector shows the active class. Each class's Overview card links directly to its arena in **Arenas**.

Each registered robot has a team name, a robot name, a weight class, and a check-in status, plus an optional weapon type (such as **Vertical spinner**) and photo. Within a class, the same team can't enter two robots with the same name. Managers can paste registration lists, edit any robot's weapon type, photo, and owner email, and export the roster (with weapon types, owners, and contact phones) as CSV.

### Team registration

Each meetup has one secret registration link, `/register/<meetup>/<token>`. In **Meetup settings → Team registration**, staff open or close registration (it starts closed and works while the meetup is still a draft), copy the link, print a sheet with its QR code for the venue door, and **Regenerate link** if it was shared too widely. A new link stops the old one from accepting new robots straight away.

A team opens the link on a phone and continues with Google. The first time, it gives a contact phone number (required, visible only to staff) and a default team name. It then registers robots: team name, robot name, weight class, optional weapon type (with tappable examples), and an optional photo. Registered robots go straight onto the class roster, not checked in, with a **Team** tag that shows the owner's email and phone to staff; check-in stays at the staff desk. Teams can edit a robot's names, weapon type, and photo at any time. Until its class starts they can also move it to another class or cancel its registration; after that, staff withdraw it. One Google account can register up to five robots in a meetup, counting robots staff assigned to it; at the limit the page says so in place of **Add a robot**, and staff can still add more for that team in **Robots & classes**. An account can make up to 100 changes and upload up to 30 photos a day, and at most 60 saves in an hour; past that the page asks it to try again later. Photos can be uploaded only through a registration link that is open, or by a team that already has robots in that meetup. Each class stops accepting robots at its first battle or at 64 robots, and registration closes when the meetup is complete. A team that already has robots in the meetup can always open its page, from the old link or the new one, to edit them, even after registration closes. A team with no robots there that signs in while registration is closed (or with an old link) is told so, without being asked for its phone number.

Every robot a team registers is also saved to that Google account's **garage**, so it can be entered in a later meetup in one step. A meetup keeps its own copy: an edit made from a meetup's page updates that meetup and the saved robot, and other meetups don't change. Deleting a meetup leaves the garage alone. Staff can type a team's Google address as a robot's owner; when that team opens the registration link, the robot is theirs to edit and joins their garage.

Photos are prepared in the browser before upload: the team drags and zooms the photo in a square frame, and the page fixes phone rotation, scales it to at most 1024 pixels, and compresses it (WebP, or JPEG where the browser can't make WebP) to under 300 KB. A team can only attach a photo it uploaded itself. Photos are private until the meetup is published. Robots without a photo show a line-art mech in the Hangar HUD style, tinted per robot so two of them facing off still look different.

The registration pages open in the meetup's display language and have an **EN / עב** toggle that the browser remembers for that meetup. In Hebrew they read right to left.

### Attendance

- **No-show before the bracket.** Only checked-in robots enter the bracket. **Generate bracket** lists the robots it leaves out, and the class roster in **Robots & classes** marks them **Not in bracket**. Until the class's first battle starts, checking a robot in or out rebuilds the unstarted bracket.
- **Late to a battle.** In the class's queue in **Arenas**, a manager can start a **Last call** on any upcoming battle whose two robots are known. The countdown (default two minutes, set per meetup in **Meetup settings**) shows on the staff **Arenas** view and Overview card, the public arena page, Ringmaster view, and the arena's in-battle screen. Starting the battle, recording its result, holding it, or cancelling the call ends it. When the countdown reaches zero, staff can record a forfeit with one click for whichever robot is present. One last call can run per arena.
- **Leaving mid-event.** After the class's first battle, **Withdraw** on the robot's **Check-in** row forfeits every remaining battle for that robot to its opponent, including losers-bracket battles it would reach later. Its earlier results stay, it can no longer be checked in, and ladders and brackets mark it **Withdrawn**. A robot can't be withdrawn while it is fighting.
- **Late arrival.** Entries close when a class's first battle starts or its first result is saved. Robots can no longer be added, removed, or reseeded, and the class roster in **Robots & classes** says so. Team registration for that class closes at the same time.

Each match is robot vs. robot within one weight class. Arenas operate independently and can each run one battle at a time.

Managers can activate or deactivate an arena from **Overview** or the class's **Arena** view. Deactivating an arena pauses its current clock and preserves its bracket and queue. Ringmaster view marks it inactive and removes its current battle from the live section. Dedicated arena screens show **Arena inactive**. Reactivating the arena opens it for battles and lets the manager resume a paused clock.

Managers can remove a class from **Robots & classes**. After confirmation, removing a class also removes its arena, roster, bracket, and results from the meetup. Its assigned venue displays switch to Ringmaster view and keep their links. Completed meetups must be reopened before removing a class.

Super admins can select **Fill with demo data** in **Robots & classes** to practise with an open meetup. It asks how many classes to add, up to the 12-class limit, and how many robots go in each, from 2 to 64. The defaults are two classes with 18 and 6 robots. Each new class gets a common weight-class name the meetup doesn't use yet, its own arena, and a roster of checked-in robots with unique team names. The new classes are added alongside existing ones, and no brackets are generated, so the format is still chosen in **Generate bracket**. Other managers don't see the action.

## Brackets and match order

Each weight class has a separate bracket. Managers choose single elimination, double elimination, or **Rounds (2 losses out)** when adding a class or generating its bracket. Generating again uses the current roster seeds and replaces the unstarted pairings. When a team has more than one robot in a class, seeding keeps its robots out of the same opening battle whenever the numbers allow it, and spreads them across the bracket so they meet as late as possible; it moves the lower-seeded teammate to the nearest seed that works, so the order staff chose changes as little as possible. The class's format becomes fixed after its first battle starts or its first result is saved. When the entrant count isn't a power of two, robots that would face a bye skip the opening round and start in the next one, so the bracket (both sides in double elimination) only lists battles that will be fought. For example, 18 robots open with two play-in battles. Older brackets that still hold bye positions show **Auto advance** beside that robot and skip the position in the arena queue. In double elimination, a first loss sends a robot to the losers bracket and a second loss eliminates it. If the losers-bracket finalist wins the first grand final, a reset match decides the winner. Final results group tied places.

In **Rounds (2 losses out)**, every robot with fewer than two losses fights once per round, and a second loss eliminates it. Round 1 pairs the top half of the seeds against the bottom half (seed 1 against the first seed of the bottom half, and so on). Each later round is paired automatically and fills in as battles end. When a battle ends, its robots are paired straight away with waiting robots on the same record they haven't met. Everyone left over is paired when the current round's last battle is decided. Current-round battles stay first in the arena queue, so an early next-round battle only runs ahead of them when they're held or waiting for check-in. Pairing follows these rules:
- Robots are paired with others on the same record (unbeaten against unbeaten, one loss against one loss). An odd group sends one robot to face the next record group.
- Rematches, and battles between two robots from the same team, are avoided whenever another pairing allows it.
- With an odd number of robots left, one robot sits the round out. The sit-out goes to a robot with the fewest sit-outs so far, so no robot sits out twice before every other live robot has sat out once. While the next round fills in early, one robot that is due a sit-out is held back until the current round ends whenever a sit-out might be needed.

Robots are never more than one round apart, and every live robot has fought the same number of battles, give or take one, at each round, so the finalists arrive with near-equal battle counts. When two robots remain, their battle is the **Final**. If the robot with one loss wins it, both have one loss and a **Final decider** follows. The last robot standing wins, and the others place by the round they went out in, with ties sharing a place.

Only the current round and the part of the next round already paired are known in advance; displays note that more pairings are added as the current round's battles end. A standings table lists each robot's wins and losses, battles fought, and status. Managers can swap unstarted slots within the current round, including the sit-out. Correcting a result that changes the winner drops the later rounds and pairs them again. If a later battle has already started, a super admin must confirm that rollback. A withdrawn robot forfeits its current battle and is not paired again.

Managers can edit the ladder before and during the event. They can swap unstarted slots within the same class, bracket side, and round, and can also trade any two robot seeds in the winners bracket (for example, moving a robot that skipped the opening round into a play-in battle). They preview the downstream changes before saving. Started matches keep their entrants. Managers can also change the order of upcoming matches in the class's arena. The next battle is the first queued matchup with two confirmed, available robots. A manager can hold a match to allow the next ready match to proceed.

Managers start battles and record a winner. The outcome records a win after a played battle or a forfeit when the other robot concedes or cannot compete. Either outcome advances the winner. Saving a result advances the bracket and updates the queue together. Managers can correct a result when the affected later matches are unstarted. If an affected match has started, a super admin reviews and confirms the rollback. The event log records the original result, the correction, and the staff member responsible.

## Spectator pages and displays

| View | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Ringmaster view | Current battles and countdowns in active arenas (with robot photos) at the top, all weight-class brackets in the middle, and the next ready battle or status for each arena at the bottom. |
| All ladders | Every weight class's bracket arranged by round as a wired tree (each battle sits between the battles that feed it, and the live battle's wire is highlighted), including inactive arenas' brackets, with matchups, winners, and links to class details. |
| Single arena | Current battle with robot photos, next ready battle, and arena status. |
| Bracket | Class selector, matchups by round, results, standings (rounds format), and placements. |
| In battle | Full-screen robot photos, team and robot names, and weapon types, arena and weight class, and the selected arena's countdown. |

Wherever a battle or a last call is shown (In battle, Single arena, Ringmaster view, and the staff **Arenas**, **Check-in**, and **Robots & classes** views), each robot appears with its photo or mech placeholder and its weapon type. Venue screens load the photos of each arena's current and next battle ahead of time. In the In battle and Ringmaster view face-offs and in the staff **Overview**, **Arenas**, **Check-in**, and **Robots & classes** views, robot names wrap between words, and a name whose longest word doesn't fit its space is shown in a smaller font instead of being split; only a word too wide even at the smallest size breaks inside the word.

Each display follows its configured layout and shows the event name beside BattleScene in its header. Wide spectator views fit the available TV or projector screen without scrolling. Phone views keep their normal scroll layout. Managers can configure up to 10 named displays per meetup and switch each display's layout from the dashboard. A new meetup starts with one display named Ringmaster in Ringmaster view. It is not marked as the spectator view, and managers can edit or remove it like any other display. Single-bracket displays can focus on specific rounds so text stays legible across the venue.

In **Displays**, managers can mark one display as **Spectator view** and copy the public meetup link. **Open display** on that row opens the display's own direct link, which shows the same view and is the one to use on a venue screen. Changing the designation updates what the same public link opens. When no display is designated, the public link opens Ringmaster view. Each named display also has a direct link that stays stable as its layout changes.

A meetup theme has a logo, background color, accent color, and light or dark text. New meetups start with the dark steel background and ice-blue accent of the app's Hangar HUD look, and meetups still on the earlier amber default show the new default. Managers can preview the theme in phone and widescreen layouts. QR actions have a title and destination URL. A meetup-wide QR can be overridden at the arena or display level. On phones, the same action is tappable.

Each meetup has a display language, English or Hebrew, chosen when the meetup is created and changeable at any time in **Meetup settings**. Existing meetups are English. The language applies to spectator pages, venue displays, and the display previews in the dashboard. The staff dashboard, sign-in, and error messages stay in English. Hebrew pages read right to left throughout: ladders and brackets run from round 1 on the right toward the final on the left, face-offs and wires mirror, and clocks, scores, and battle ids still read left to right. Names that staff type (meetup, classes, arenas, robots, teams, QR titles) appear as entered. Hebrew text uses Rubik beside Oxanium and Assistant beside Rajdhani and Share Tech Mono, so the Hangar HUD look carries over, and English names on a Hebrew page keep the original fonts. Hebrew robot names on English pages use the same Hebrew fonts.

Every screen ends with the developer credit, “© <current year> Developed by Udi Tirosh”, linking to https://www.iollama.com/. This includes the staff dashboard, sign-in, spectator pages, and venue displays (in Hebrew on Hebrew pages). After it comes a **Get your own BattleScene** link to the public project, https://github.com/iollama/BattleScene, which opens in a new tab. Venue display links (a display's own link and the In battle link) leave that link out, since nobody can click a TV. On wide venue displays the credit fits within the screen without scrolling. Display previews inside the dashboard leave it out.

The staff sign-in page has **Sign in with Google** at the right of the header, on the same row as the BattleScene logo. Below it, the page shows the BattleScene banner (the README's image: what the app does and that it's free), so a visitor who isn't staff can see what the app is. A Google account without staff access gets a message above the banner and a **Sign out** button in place of **Sign in with Google**.

## Battle timer

Each arena has an independent countdown. The default is three minutes, configurable by meetup and adjustable before a battle. Managers can start, pause, resume, and reset the clock. Opening or refreshing a screen mid-battle reconstructs the countdown from the last saved timer state. When the timer reaches zero, the screen shows **Time up** and the manager enters the result.

A disconnected screen keeps displaying the last confirmed state and marks the connection as stale. It resynchronizes on reconnect.

## Hosting and data

Deploy on Vercel with one private Vercel Blob store, linked with either a read-write token or the project's OIDC identity. The app creates meetup records on demand and persists event data and roles there. Google OAuth provides staff identity. The initial deployment needs Google OAuth credentials, a session secret, an initial super admin account, and the linked Blob store.

Public screens share a cached feed of published fields. Staff data, permissions, drafts, robot owners, and contact phones stay private. Uploaded images (the theme logo and robot photos) are public only once the meetup is published. Public images are cached at the edge for five minutes and in browsers for a day; unpublishing or deleting a meetup, or replacing an image, clears the edge copy. Teams' saved robots and photos are stored per Google account, apart from any meetup. The countdown ticks locally from saved timestamps. Concurrent writes that would overwrite an intervening change are rejected, with the latest state available for review.

The staff dashboard reloads the open meetup every 30 seconds to show other people's changes. A dashboard that nobody has touched for 10 minutes, on a meetup nobody has changed for 30, reloads every 5 minutes, and after 2 hours of both every 30 minutes. Touching the page reloads it at once, and a meetup that is being run always keeps the 30 seconds. Venue displays and spectator pages are not slowed.

Reads and writes of stored data are what the hosting plan rations, so the app spends them only on real use:

- Requests from visitors who aren't signed in don't add storage reads, however many arrive. A meetup id that doesn't exist is refused from a list kept with the staff roles, and everything else they can ask for is answered from a copy the server already holds, apart from the display feed described next.
- A signed-in team account is rationed: its daily limits above, and at most 20 requests an hour for photos that don't exist.
- The display feed is read from storage once per saved change, plus a check every 20 seconds while the meetup is in use (changed in the last two hours). After that the check costs nothing, so a screen left on does too.
- The staff dashboard's 30-second reload is answered from the server's copy when that copy is under 30 seconds old. **Refresh**, and the reload after a conflict, always read the latest.
- Vercel can run more than one copy of the server at a time. A change saved through one copy (manager access granted or revoked, publishing or unpublishing, a new photo, a regenerated registration link, or the first change after two quiet hours) can take up to about two minutes to show through another. Photos of a meetup that was just unpublished can stay reachable for up to five more minutes. With one copy, the usual case, these are immediate. Every save, on any copy, still reads the latest data first.

Vercel Hobby is the starting tier for personal, noncommercial use. At the planned size, caching public data is important to stay within the included Blob operations: by estimate an event day uses about a third of the monthly reads and half of the writes, which leaves room for about two event days a month. Actual usage depends on the number and duration of events and viewers. ## Acceptance criteria

1. A manager creates a meetup and registers up to 30 robots across three classes. A manager assigned to a different meetup cannot access the draft.
2. One, two, or three arenas can be active at a time. A result or activity change affects only the relevant class and arena. A paused clock can be resumed after reactivating its arena.
3. A manager reorders an arena queue and swaps unstarted bracket slots after a battle starts. Started matches and their results remain intact.
4. Single and double elimination handle byes, forfeits, and result corrections. Double elimination handles a grand-final reset. In the rounds format, every live robot's battle count stays within one of the others at each round, and the next round fills in automatically as battles end. Robots that aren't checked in stay out of a generated bracket, a last call ends in a one-click forfeit, and a withdrawn robot forfeits all of its remaining battles.
5. Up to 10 venue screens can show independent views, including arena-specific timers. Ringmaster view and All ladders fit on a single landscape screen. Managers can change display settings, branding, and QR actions. The public meetup link follows the display marked **Spectator view** and falls back to Ringmaster view when that designation is cleared.
6. Under normal connectivity, saved changes appear on venue displays (opened with **Open display** or **In-battle screen**) within 3 seconds and on other spectator pages, such as phones on the public link, within 6 seconds. The load target is 50 simultaneous phone viewers and three venue screens.
7. Simultaneous staff saves preserve the confirmed result. Unexpected connection loss is visible and the screen recovers when service returns.
8. With registration open, a team on a phone signs in with Google through the meetup's link and registers two robots in the same class, one with a photo taken on the phone; both appear on the roster, and the photo shows square on the In battle screen once the meetup is published. After **Regenerate link**, the old link accepts no new robots but still lets that team edit its robots. In a later meetup, the team enters a saved robot from its garage without retyping it. The registration pages work in English and in Hebrew (right to left).
9. Switching a meetup's display language to Hebrew turns every spectator view and venue display right to left in Hebrew within the normal refresh time, with round 1 of each ladder on the right; switching back restores the English layout.
